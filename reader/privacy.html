Privacy

What Reader reads from your Google account, where it keeps it, and what never leaves your browser.

Reader is a reader for research papers: it searches the indexes, keeps the papers you collect in your own Google Drive, and shows your Google Calendar beside them. It has no database of its own. Your library is yours, in your Drive; the only server involved is a small proxy that fetches PDFs and search results the browser cannot fetch directly.

Signing in with Google

Signing in asks Google for your name, email address and picture, to show who is signed in and to name the library in Drive as yours. The sign-in token Google issues lasts about an hour and is held in the browser's session storage, never written to disk by the app and never stored on a server. Signing out revokes it.

Your email address is sent to the proxy once per sign-in, so it can issue a short-lived pass for the PDF and search routes and count what each account uses of the site's own API keys. Those per-account totals are visible only to the person running this copy of Reader.

Google Drive

Reader asks for the drive.file and drive.appdata scopes. Under these the app can only see files it created itself: a Papers_collection folder in your Drive holding your library (library.json), the PDFs you save, and your notes. It cannot read anything else in your Drive. Nothing from your Drive is copied to a server; PDFs fetched through the proxy go straight to your browser and are saved by it into your Drive.

Google Calendar

The Calendar page asks for the read-only calendar.readonly scope, only when you choose to connect it. Reader reads the list of your calendars and the events in the span on screen, and shows them in the browser. Calendar data is never sent to or stored on any server, and nothing in Reader writes to your calendar. If you tag a meeting with one of your projects, the tag (the meeting's id and title) is kept in library.json in your own Drive. Forget in Settings drops the connection in that browser; you can revoke it for good at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Google Colab

If you connect Colab to run code, Reader asks for the colaboratory scope and uses it to start and talk to a Colab runtime as you. Notebooks and outputs are kept in your Drive.

AI features

Ask AI and Explain send the text of the paper you are reading, and your question, to the model you pick (Anthropic, DeepSeek or Google Gemini), either with a key you enter or through the proxy with the site's keys. Nothing is sent until you use one of these features. Their answers can be kept in your notes, in your Drive.

Limited use

Reader's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide the features described here, is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone other than the services you choose to use.

What stays in your browser

Settings, the page you were on, and preferences such as the calendar view live in the browser's local storage. A copy of your library is cached in the browser's IndexedDB, one per account, so the app opens quickly; Drive's copy is the one that counts.

Reader is open source. Questions about this policy go to the contact email shown on Google's sign-in screen for this app.
